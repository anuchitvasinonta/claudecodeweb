## The constraint that actually binds

Not the build. AI makes the build the cheap half now. For one person the
binding constraints are:

- **Distribution** — can it be sold without a salesperson?
- **Support load** — does customer number 200 cost you an evening?
- **Payment friction** — can someone pay without a contract and an invoice?

Every candidate is annotated against these, because they're what decides
whether a thing is a solo business or a job.

## Group A — no advantage from your background

Generated deliberately without reference to your history. These are the purest
test of "I can build and ship alone."

### A1. Listing generator for Thai marketplace sellers

Product photos and copy for Shopee / Lazada / TikTok Shop sellers: upload a
phone photo, get clean product shots, a Thai title, bullet points and variant
copy that fit each platform's format.

- **Who'd pay:** small sellers and one-person brands. Very large population,
  already spending money on ads, immediately understands the ROI.
- **Runs on:** web app, pay-per-use or a small monthly credit pack.
- **Why solo works:** entirely self-serve, no onboarding call, no contract.
- **What kills it:** the platforms ship it themselves as a free feature. Real
  risk. Also low switching cost — you're only ever as good as last month's
  model.
- **Size:** small. Shippable slice in weeks.

### A2. Receipts to books for Thai SMEs

Owner photographs receipts as they happen; the service extracts, categorises
and hands the accountant a clean monthly file instead of a shoebox.

- **Who'd pay:** SME owners, or the accounting firms who'd rather receive
  structured data. Two possible buyers for the same product — worth testing
  which one pays faster.
- **Runs on:** capture is the whole battle, so this is a genuine LINE case —
  no install, already open on the phone. Reports on web.
- **Why solo works:** recurring by nature, and the work happens monthly whether
  you're there or not.
- **What kills it:** Thai receipt formats are chaotic and accuracy has to be
  near-perfect before anyone trusts it with tax. Long unglamorous grind.
- **Size:** medium. The last 10% of accuracy is most of the work.

### A3. Micro-SaaS for a global niche, priced in USD

A narrow tool inside somebody else's ecosystem — a marketplace app for a
platform that already has buyers with credit cards on file.

- **Who'd pay:** global SMBs, none of whom will ever know who you are.
- **Runs on:** web plus the host marketplace, Stripe, recurring.
- **Why solo works:** the marketplace *is* the distribution. This is the only
  candidate here where you don't have to solve distribution yourself.
- **What kills it:** platform dependency, and you're competing with everyone on
  earth who had the same idea. Also the least connected to anything you know,
  which cuts both ways.
- **Size:** small to medium, but picking the right niche is most of the value
  and can't be rushed.

## Group B — uses a skill you have, not a contact

The DSP background is the rarest thing on your list. Almost nobody currently
building AI applications can do signal processing, and the overlap is where
problems stay unsolved because the AI-app crowd can't reach them.

### B1. Call review for Thai SMEs

Every recorded call scored instead of the 2% a human supervisor samples:
what was promised, what was missed, which agents need coaching, which calls
need a follow-up.

- **Who'd pay:** insurance agencies, clinics, property agents, delivery firms —
  anyone whose revenue rides on phone conversations.
- **Runs on:** web dashboard, plugged into whatever records their calls.
- **Why solo works:** recurring, priced per seat or per hour of audio, and the
  value is obvious in the first week.
- **What kills it:** Thai ASR quality on bad phone audio, and the fact that
  this needs a light integration per customer — which is the support load
  problem in disguise.
- **Size:** medium.

### B2. Acoustic anomaly detection for small facilities

A microphone on a pump, compressor, chiller or lift; the service learns what
healthy sounds like and reports when it changes. Condition monitoring for
people who can't afford real condition monitoring.

- **Who'd pay:** building managers, small factories, cold-chain operators.
- **Runs on:** a cheap mic plus edge box, service and alerting on web. This is
  the "a bit of hardware is acceptable" case — small, per-site, not inventory.
- **Why solo works:** genuinely defensible. The DSP-plus-ML combination is the
  moat, and there's real recurring revenue per monitored machine.
- **What kills it:** every deployment is a site visit unless you make it truly
  plug-in-and-go, and that's a hardware design problem you said you don't want.
  Longest path to first payment of anything here.
- **Size:** large. Be honest about that.

## Group C — uses a contact

Flagged because contacts make the first sale easy and the *business* hard.
Read `notes.md` on what "alone" is meant to prove before leaning here.

### C1. Adaptive training plans for Thai runners

A coach that actually reads your week — what you ran, what you skipped, how it
felt — and rewrites next week instead of handing you a fixed 16-week PDF.

- **Who'd pay:** amateur runners chasing a first half or full marathon. You can
  reach them.
- **Runs on:** strong LINE case — daily check-ins belong in chat, not an app
  nobody opens twice.
- **Why solo works:** pure B2C self-serve, matches your stated preference, no
  invoicing, no accounts.
- **What kills it:** consumer willingness to pay is thin, churn spikes the week
  after race day, and free training plans are everywhere.
- **Note:** overlaps your own dormant health aspect. That's motivating and also
  a way to blur two projects together — worth naming if this advances.
- **Size:** small to medium.

### C2. Hospital discharge follow-up — flagged as a trap

The contacts are real and the problem is real. But hospital procurement, PDPA,
security review and a 9-month sales cycle are the exact opposite of solo. You'd
spend a year selling before you learned whether anyone uses it.

### C3. Security systems for police/military — flagged as a trap

Reselling vendor systems means hardware stock, government procurement and
relationship maintenance. It contradicts three things you stated in the same
message: software service, no money sunk in inventory, handled by yourself.
Your best-connected door leads away from what you said you want.
